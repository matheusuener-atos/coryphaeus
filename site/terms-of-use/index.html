1. What PAULUS is

PAULUS is a Windows desktop program that works as an artificial-intelligence legal assistant. It runs on the office's machine and brings together a document archive, editor, calendar, digital signatures, email, contacts, finances and an Approvals queue. The AI is part of a subscription (section 7): with it, answers are written in the cloud, and a local AI model (Ollama) answers as a backup; without it, the program works without AI.

Documents, messages and answers stay on your computer; no PAULUS server receives them, except the text that passes through the PAULUS cloud, if the principal turns it on (section 7), which is not stored. The program checks the paulus.ia.br website for new versions and, on the Open development screen, reads the public history; if the office subscribes to the cloud plan, the website passes the payment to Mercado Pago (section 7). For email and Google services, the program connects directly to your provider (Google, Microsoft or other).

6. Remote access (optional)

The office can turn on remote access, on its own computer, to use PAULUS from home or a phone. It comes turned off. When on:

  • PAULUS and the documents stay on the office computer, which must be on with the program open; what travels over the internet is the screen;
  • the route is a Cloudflare tunnel, at an <office>.paulus.ia.br address created in the project's Cloudflare account. Midway, traffic passes unencrypted through Cloudflare; we do not route, inspect or log that content (see the Privacy Policy);
  • the office is responsible for the accounts it creates and the security level it chooses for each one (every person passes an anti-bot check and signs in with their own account and, depending on that level, an authenticator code), for keeping recovery codes, and for turning off or removing access when no longer needed;
  • signing, moving or deleting files, exporting the archive and changing settings remain only on the office computer; what is proposed remotely in the calendar, tasks and contacts goes through the Approvals queue;
  • every access is logged on the office computer;
  • the office may let chat answers be written on the device of the person asking (it comes turned off; only the account holder turns it on, on the office computer, for the office and account by account, and can mark matters that stay only at the office). The office is responsible for deciding who may use it and in which matters; each person is responsible for the security of the device they use. The text coming back from the device is checked at the office, but it is still an AI answer, subject to the professional's review (item 3). What goes to the device, what stays on it and what cannot be guaranteed is in the Privacy Policy;
  • the paulus.ia.br address is offered while there are slots in this phase, with no guarantee of continuous availability; an address never connected within 7 days, or idle for more than 180, is released automatically.

7. Cloud (optional, paid)

The office's principal can turn on, on the office computer, the cloud: conversation answers, summaries and drafting are then written by an AI model outside the computer. It comes turned off and only turns on after the principal reads the terms and says yes. When on:

  • with PAULUS (cloud), the text passes through the paulus.ia.br website and is processed by the provider of the plan's model: DeepInfra (Llama, in the United States) on Advogado, Mistral AI (in France) on Escritório and Anthropic (Claude, in the United States) on Escritório Plus. With the office's own API key (DeepInfra, Anthropic or OpenAI), it goes straight to the provider, under the office's contract with it. What goes, what never goes and what is kept is in the Privacy policy;
  • there are three plans — Advogado, Escritório and Escritório Plus — and each has its own AI model, monthly credit quota, number of people, maximum depth and the program features it unlocks (for example, team, service invoices, DataJud, meeting recording, Word); the price and what each includes are shown on the paulus.ia.br/assinatura page and in the program before subscribing. The subscription is made through it (with sign-up at paulus.ia.br/cadastro) or in PAULUS itself; sign-up asks for the office name, CPF or CNPJ, address, phone and bar (OAB) number, and the subscriber is responsible for their accuracy;
  • payment is through Mercado Pago, in two ways: monthly, charged to the card every month, or yearly, the year paid at once, in full or in up to 12 card instalments, with any instalment interest paid by whoever chooses instalments. On the yearly plan the quota is still monthly (each month has its own), plan changes happen at renewal, and the plan does not renew by itself: renewal opens 45 days before the end;
  • the credit quota is monthly, released weekly (about 7/30 of it each week), and does not carry over from one week to the next. Once a month, the next week's quota can be brought forward, and that week is then empty — never in the first 7 days of the subscription. Each model spends credits at its own rate: on Escritório Plus, Claude Opus, used at the Ministro level, spends twice as much. When the quota runs out, the office can top up by Pix, at the plan's price, and a top-up does not expire at renewal. Without a plan in good standing, the program works without AI and with the simplest plan's features; with the plan in good standing but no credits left, or without internet, answers are written on the computer, with the local model, until the top-up, the next week or the renewal;
  • right of withdrawal: in the first 7 days after the first purchase (Brazilian Consumer Protection Code, art. 49), the office may withdraw and gets back what it paid, by writing to contato@paulus.ia.br; the plan ends when the refund is made;
  • cancelling the monthly subscription, in Settings › Models, stops the following charges; the credits of a cycle already paid remain valid until it ends. The yearly plan has nothing to cancel: it lasts until the end of the paid year. Billing questions: contato@paulus.ia.br;
  • the office is the controller of its clients' data and is responsible for having a legal basis to send it to the cloud and for telling its clients that AI is used; an answer written in the cloud is still an AI answer, subject to the professional's review (section 3);
  • the cloud is offered in this phase without a guarantee of continuous availability: the provider may be down, and the answer is then written on the computer.
