Summary

  • PAULUS is a desktop program for Windows that runs on the law office's own computer. The AI is part of a subscription: with it, answers are written in the cloud, which comes turned off and only turns on after the office's principal says yes, and a local model answers as a backup without internet or when the quota runs out. Without the subscription, the program works without AI.
  • No PAULUS server receives your messages, documents or client data. Our only server is the one behind this website, and it only receives something from you in the optional features: the cloud through the PAULUS plan, where the text passes through and is not stored (see Cloud), model calibration (see Other internet access) and remote access (see Remote access).
  • If you connect a Google account, PAULUS accesses the account's email address and your Gmail messages to show your inbox, suggest deadlines for your calendar, and send the replies you approve.
  • If you also connect Google Calendar or Google Drive (each one only when you ask), PAULUS copies your appointments to your Google Calendar, creates their Google Meet rooms, sends to your Drive the documents you approve and, if you authorize Drive reading, keeps a local copy of the Drive folders you choose.
  • Google data is not used for advertising, not sold, not used to train AI models, and not shared with third parties.
  • You can turn everything off at any time: remove the account in PAULUS and revoke access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

How PAULUS works

  • PAULUS is installed on and runs on the office's computer (Windows).
  • The local AI model runs through Ollama at the internal address 127.0.0.1 — that is, on the machine itself — and, with the subscription, answers as a backup. Questions, documents and the assistant's answers only go to an AI service on the internet through the cloud, which only turns on after the office's principal says yes (see Cloud).
  • No part of the program sends usage data, documents or messages to us. The exception is an action you take, described in Other internet access: sending feedback.

How the data is used

Google data is used only to provide you, inside the program, with these features:

  1. Show your inbox and the messages you open in PAULUS.
  2. Suggest calendar deadlines: when a message mentions a deadline, PAULUS proposes an appointment or task, which you confirm or discard.
  3. Prepare and send replies you approve: the assistant may write a draft, but sending goes through the Approvals queue and only happens with your confirmation.
  4. Organize the mailbox when you ask: archive, delete (the message goes to your provider's trash), mark as read or unread, and star.
  5. Save an attachment to the Archive, when you choose to save it.
  6. Copy your appointments to Google Calendar, with sync turned on, and create the Meet room of an online appointment when you ask (asking for the room sends that appointment to your Google Calendar, even with sync off); and show your Google Calendar events inside the program.
  7. Upload documents to your Google Drive, in the PAULUS folder: each upload goes through the Approvals queue and only happens with your confirmation.
  8. Keep in the Archive a copy of the Drive folders you choose, updated every 15 minutes, so you can search those documents on your computer.

This processing — including reading by the AI to understand a message, detect deadlines and write drafts, and the translation of messages in English or Spanish — happens on your computer, with local models. Google data does not go to the cloud described in Cloud: email, its saved attachments and the copy of Drive folders are left out of it.

Limited Use (Google)

PAVLVS's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy (https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy), including the Limited Use requirements.

In practice, data received from Google:

  • is used only for the user-facing features described above;
  • is not used for advertising, including personalized or retargeted advertising;
  • is not sold;
  • is not used to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models;
  • is not transferred to or shared with third parties;
  • is not read by humans, except with your explicit consent for a specific message (for example, when you forward it to us yourself), when needed for security purposes or to comply with the law.

Where data is stored and how it is protected

  • Messages travel only between your computer and your email provider (Google, Microsoft or other), over an encrypted connection. They do not pass through any PAULUS server.
  • Calendar and Drive: the appointments and documents you send go directly from your computer to your Google account, over an encrypted connection, and stay in your Calendar and Drive, managed by Google. The Drive folders you choose to copy come directly from Google to your computer. None of this passes through a PAULUS server.
  • Remote images: an email may request images hosted on the sender's servers. PAULUS blocks those images by default. If you choose to show them (in a message, or in Email › Settings), the computer downloads them from those servers, like any email program — which may tell the sender the message was opened.
  • What the program stores stays on the computer itself, in the PAULUS folders.
  • The Google authorization (the refresh token, which lets the program stay connected) is encrypted by the Windows account (DPAPI): only the same user account, on that computer, can decrypt it.
  • The short-lived access token is kept only in memory while the program is open; it is never written to disk.
  • Physical and access protection of the computer (Windows password, antivirus, backups) is the responsibility of whoever administers it.

Sharing

We do not share, sell or transfer Google data, or any other data you handle in PAULUS, to third parties. The necessary exchange is with your own email provider, to read and send your messages — for example Google, when the account is Gmail — and, if you connect them, with your own Google Calendar and Google Drive, where the appointments and documents you chose go, and from where the Drive folders you chose are copied.

Outside email there are two exceptions, both voluntary and described in Cloud: if the principal turns the cloud on, the text sent goes to the model provider; and, if the office subscribes to the PAULUS (cloud) plan, the payer's email and the amount go to Mercado Pago, which processes the payment. Google data never enters the payment.

When you send an approved message, it goes to the recipients you chose, as in any email program.

Other internet access

  • Legislation: PAULUS accesses the public website planalto.gov.br to download the text of laws. This access sends no personal data.
  • Case law and case lookups (when you ask): in Library › Case law, PAULUS downloads the STJ (Superior Court of Justice) decisions of the panels you choose, from the court's open data (dadosabertos.web.stj.jus.br); the list of repetitive-appeal themes comes from the same place. Looking up a case by its number queries the public DataJud API of the CNJ (api-publica.datajud.cnj.jus.br) with only that number. No document or office data goes along.
  • CNPJ typed into a record: when a complete CNPJ (Brazilian company tax ID) is typed into a CNPJ field (Records, My details, invoice), PAULUS queries BrasilAPI (brasilapi.com.br), which returns the public Federal Revenue data (company name, address, phone, e-mail), to fill in the record's empty fields. Only the CNPJ number is sent; no other record or office data. CPF (individual tax ID) is never looked up.
  • Court publications (optional): if you turn on the daily check of court publications, PAULUS queries the public API of Brazil's national judicial electronic gazette (comunicaapi.pje.jus.br) with only the lawyer's bar number (OAB) and state.
  • WhatsApp Web: if you want, the program opens WhatsApp Web inside it. You then use WhatsApp's service directly, subject to WhatsApp's terms and privacy policy.
  • Other email providers (Microsoft or other): the same rules of this policy apply — messages travel only between your computer and the provider.
  • New version: once a day, PAULUS reads the file paulus.ia.br/atualizacao.json to know whether there is a new version. That read is all: no document, office data, name or email goes with it (the website, like any website, sees the internet address of whoever accesses it). If there is a new version, the official installer is downloaded from the project's GitHub and checked by SHA-256 before opening. The check comes on and can be turned off in the setup assistant or in Settings › Version.
  • Model downloads: the first time you use them, PAULUS downloads the speech model for Recordings (Whisper, from the public Hugging Face repository) and the email translation packages (from argos-net.com). Language models are downloaded when you ask, in Settings › Models, from Ollama's public registry (registry.ollama.ai). The installer may download Ollama from ollama.com, Cloudflare's cloudflared from its official GitHub (the remote-access program, off until the office turns it on), and Microsoft's WebView2. These are file downloads only; no document, audio, text or data of yours is sent.
  • Model calibration (optional, off by default): if you turn on "Participate in calibration", the program sends to paulus.ia.br this machine's measurements — processor, memory, the two measured speeds and how many words per second each model writes and reads — and downloads other machines' measurements, to better estimate how long each model takes. Nothing from the office is sent: no document, name or email. The screen shows exactly what is sent beforehand. Turning it off stops sending immediately.
  • Feedback (optional): in Settings › Feedback, PAULUS opens an email addressed to contato@paulus.ia.br with what you wrote. You review it before it is sent, and sending goes through the Approvals queue like any email.
  • Links that open in the browser: some buttons only open a page in your browser — for example Google sign-in, the Ollama download page and the new-meeting page of Google Meet or Microsoft Teams. What happens there follows each site's rules.

Remote access (optional)

PAULUS can be used from home or from a phone, with the program running on the office computer. This module comes turned off, and only the office turns it on and off, on the computer itself.

  • What stays on the office computer: documents, the index and what PAULUS extracted from them; and the AI model, unless the office turns on writing on the device (see the next section). They do not go to paulus.ia.br or to us. What travels over the internet is the program's screen and what is typed into it.
  • How it travels: through a Cloudflare tunnel, at an <office>.paulus.ia.br address created in the project's Cloudflare account. The connection is encrypted between your browser and Cloudflare and between Cloudflare and the office computer, but Cloudflare opens it midway to deliver it: in that stretch, traffic passes unencrypted through Cloudflare, which handles it under its own privacy policy. We do not route, inspect or log that content.
  • Who gets in: only people who pass the anti-bot check (Cloudflare Turnstile) and sign in with their own PAULUS account — with Google (or email and password) and, depending on the security level the office chooses for that account, the code from an authenticator app. Signing documents, moving or deleting files, exporting the archive and changing settings remain only on the office computer.
  • Log: every remote access is logged on the office computer — who signed in, when, what they opened and downloaded — for one year, for the office itself to consult.

Writing the answer on the device (optional)

With remote access on, the office can let the answer to a question be written by the computer (or phone) of the person asking, instead of the office computer — useful when the office is busy with other questions. It comes turned off. Only the account holder turns it on, on the office computer, for the office and account by account, and can mark clients, matters and folders whose content never goes to any device. Turning it off takes effect immediately: whatever was being written is finished at the office.

  • What stays at the office: searching the documents, the index, and everything that means reading a whole document, editing, drafting, email, OCR and adding documents. The feature applies only to chat answers written from excerpts.
  • What goes to the device, for each question: the question, the start of the conversation, the assistant's instructions and the excerpts of the documents that answer it — excerpts only, never a whole document, up to about 9,000 characters, and only from documents that account could already open through remote access. It travels through the same remote-access tunnel, from the office computer to the person's browser, with the same Cloudflare stretch described above; no PAULUS server and no internet AI service receives anything.
  • What goes to the device, once: the file of an AI model of up to 3 GB that is on the office computer (by default Llama 3.2 3B, about 2 GB) and the program that runs it in the browser, both served by the office computer and checked against a fingerprint (SHA-256) before use. The model file is the same for everyone: it contains nothing from the office.
  • What is kept on the device: only the model file, in the browser's storage, until the person deletes it (My account › This device) or the browser clears it; and, in the browser, a few preferences of this screen: the choice between office, device and automatic, the speed-test numbers and “don't suggest again today”. The question, the excerpts and the answer are not stored: they exist in memory while the answer is written and are discarded at the end.
  • What the office checks: the text coming back from the device is treated as untrusted and goes through the office's checks before it is shown or saved — numbers, dates and amounts not in the excerpts, citations of excerpts that were not sent, laws not in the excerpts, stray links and emails. If it fails, the office writes the answer again. Every answer says where it was written and why.
  • What the office receives from the device: besides the answer text, only the capacity-test numbers (whether the browser has WebGPU, memory, speed and the time to load the model). The remote-access log keeps, for one year, every hand-off and return: who, when, which documents the excerpts came from, the result of the check and a short browser code (6 characters taken from a digest of the browser's identification, only to tell apart two devices of the same person).
  • What cannot be guaranteed: a compromised device — with malware or a malicious browser extension — can read what the page shows. That is already true today, just by viewing documents through remote access. Writing the answer on the device does not increase that risk, because the device receives the same thing the person would already see on screen, but it does not remove it either. Take care of the device you use.

Cloud (optional)

The office can let conversation answers, summaries and drafting be written by a cloud AI model, larger than the one on the computer. It comes turned off, and only turns on after the office's principal reads the terms and says yes, in the office computer's own window, in Settings › Models. The yes applies to the version of the terms that was read and to the chosen provider; switching providers asks for a new yes. There are two ways:

  • PAULUS (cloud), through the office's plan: the text goes from the office computer to the paulus.ia.br website (a Cloudflare Worker), which passes it on to the company that runs the plan's model, and the answer comes back the same way. The website does not store the text: it only passes it on. Who receives it depends on the plan:
    • Advogado — DeepInfra, United States (Llama 3.3 70B). DeepInfra's published policy says it does not write to disk or log the content it receives through the API, and does not use it to train models.
    • Escritório — Mistral AI, France (Mistral Large 3). Mistral's published policy says that, on the paid API, it keeps inputs and outputs for up to 30 days to check for abuse, deletes them afterwards, and does not use them to train models.
    • Escritório Plus — Anthropic, United States (Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Opus 5.5). Anthropic's published policy says that, on the API, it deletes inputs and outputs within 30 days, except in the cases it lists (such as abuse and legal obligations), and does not use them to train models. If Anthropic declines an answer, it may itself redo it on another Claude model.
    This is each provider's policy, not technical isolation — their servers are shared with other customers. The cloud account is the account linked to PAULUS — the Atos Account; activating it confirms that account.
  • The office's own API key: with a DeepInfra, Anthropic (Claude) or OpenAI (GPT) key, contracted and paid for by the office directly with the provider. The request goes from the office computer straight to the provider; the paulus.ia.br website does not sit in between. The provider handles the text under the terms of the office's API account: reading those terms is the office's decision.

Either way there is an international transfer of personal data (LGPD, art. 33): to the United States or, on the Escritório plan, to France. The office is the controller of its clients' data: it is up to the office to have a legal basis for this and to tell its clients that AI is used.

  • What goes: in the conversation, the question, the start of the conversation, the assistant's instructions and the document excerpts the search found — the same ones that would go to the model on the computer; in summaries, the text of the matter or of the recording's transcript; in drafting, the text the person asked the AI to work on in the editor, a comment, a spreadsheet formula or the comparison with the standard contract. Each of the three can be turned off separately. Search, the index, the checks and the rest of the program stay on the computer; the conversation answer that comes back goes through the same checks as a local answer. Before the answer, a short triage sends the question and the list of names of the documents in the archive (not their content, and without the names of the ones that never go to the cloud), so the AI can say which to read and whether the question is about the law.
  • When it goes: after the principal's yes, without asking again for each question; every answer says where it was written. Whoever prefers can turn on “Ask for a yes on each question”: then each send waits in Approvals, showing the exact text that will leave. The “Cloud” pill in the question box keeps a single question on the computer. With the principal's yes, people who come in through remote access use the cloud too; with “ask on each question” on, a question from outside stays on the computer, because the yes is given in the office window.
  • Masking (on by default): CPF, CNPJ, court case numbers, email addresses and phone numbers become placeholders (“[CPF 1]”) before leaving, and are put back into the answer on the computer. This reduces exposure but does not anonymize: names, addresses and the rest of the text go as they are.
  • What never goes: email — reading, drafts, rewriting, the inbox summary; saved email attachments (marked when saved, starting with the version that brought the cloud — an attachment saved earlier has no mark; to be sure, mark its folder “office only”); the copy of Drive folders that PAULUS makes; clients, matters and folders marked “office only”; the Finance report; the conversation's quick judge and the reading of documents entering the archive. If an answer would need any of them, it is written on the computer, and the screen says why.
  • The keys: the office's API key, and the installation secret for PAULUS (cloud), stay on the computer, encrypted by the Windows account (DPAPI).
  • Log: each send is recorded on the computer, with the exact text that left, the model, the tokens and the task, in Settings › Models, and in the audit log. The yes and its withdrawal are recorded too, with the date and the version of the terms.
  • The plan and billing (PAULUS (cloud) only): the plan is monthly (a card subscription) or yearly (the year paid at once, in full or in card instalments), through Mercado Pago, and gives a monthly credit quota, released weekly; when it runs out, the office can top up by Pix, and a top-up does not expire at renewal. Without a plan in good standing, PAULUS works without AI; with the plan in good standing but no credits left, or without internet, answers are written on the computer, with the local model. The payer's email goes to Mercado Pago. The card is typed on the website's payment page, inside Mercado Pago's own secure fields: the number, expiry and code go straight to Mercado Pago, which returns to the website only a single-use code to charge. PAULUS neither sees nor stores the card data.
  • What the website keeps about the cloud account: the account's email and identifier (from Google, or from the Atos Account), the office name, the sign-up data (see Sign-up and subscription), the chosen plan, the SHA-256 digest of each installation's secret (up to three), the principal's yes (version, name and date), the subscription status and the Mercado Pago numbers for it and for top-ups, and usage numbers: tokens per cycle, used, and per day (the last 62 days). No question, document or answer text. It is kept while the account exists; to delete it, write to contato@paulus.ia.br.
  • To stop: untick “Turn on the cloud” or withdraw the yes, in Settings › Models. It takes effect immediately. Cancelling the monthly subscription stops the billing; the credits of the paid cycle remain valid until it ends. The yearly plan lasts until the end of the paid year.

Cloud administration and announcements

The PAVLVS team runs the cloud from an internal panel at paulus.ia.br/admin, open only to team members: sign-in goes through Cloudflare Access (team e-mail and a one-time code) and then a GitHub account with write access to the PAVLVS repository. The panel adds no new data; it shows what the cloud and remote access already keep. For it, the website also keeps:

  • In each cloud account: the list of confirmed payments (type, amount, date and the Mercado Pago number) and token totals per month and per model. When the team opens an account's statement, the panel asks Mercado Pago for the method of the payments the account does not keep (the yearly plan and the monthly charges from before the current card) and keeps, for 400 days, only the type (card or Pix), the brand, the last 4 digits and the installments. The Mercado Pago notices the cloud handled are kept in a short list (the 50 newest: account identifier, type, status and amount), with no name or e-mail.
  • Remote access: a short history of each address (created, first connection, address changed, deactivated, notice sent), the last 40 events; and the panel's address register: each creation, change, deactivation and release, with the address, who did it (the e-mail of whoever changed it, “the office” or “automatic cleanup”) and the reason, for 400 days.
  • To undo: each publication from the panel keeps, for 30 days, how the things it changed were before (an account's plan, billing details or pause, for example), so the team can undo it.
  • Invoices: each confirmed payment enters a queue for the service invoice (account, type, amount and date). Automatic issuing is not switched on yet.

E-mails. From the panel, the team sends service notices (a plan that did not renew, remote access that stopped) and announcements about PAVLVS to the e-mail of the cloud account. Sending is done by Resend, a United States company, which receives the address, subject and text of each e-mail. Announcements carry a 1-pixel image and a counting link so the team knows how many were opened and clicked: the website keeps only an "opened" and "clicked" mark per recipient, for up to 180 days, with no IP address or browser. The recipient list of an announcement stays on the website until sending ends and is removed afterwards; only the counts remain. To stop receiving announcements, reply to any of them asking so. The team may write to whoever did not renew and make an offer (credits or a special price on the next payment): the e-mail goes out with the writer's name, and the account keeps only when and who sent it, not the text.

Orders to an account. The panel can credit tokens, switch off an installation, cancel the subscription at Mercado Pago, and record in the account a request for PAULUS to stop using a Google service (Gmail, Calendar or Drive) or to unlink the Google account from the cloud. The owner makes the same request about Google services from My account. That request keeps only the service names, no Google data. The installed PAULUS follows it from the version that brings this feature; until then, the office switches services off in Settings. Every change to an account goes through a team confirmation and is recorded with who made it.

Service invoice (NFS-e, optional)

The office can issue Brazilian service invoices (NFS-e) through the National Standard from PAULUS. It ships turned off. It is only turned on from the office computer's window, in Settings › Invoice; from outside (remote access) the tax setup and the certificate cannot be used. Neither we nor paulus.ia.br are in the middle: requests go from the office computer straight to the National NFS-e System (gov.br).

  • What is sent: the invoice data, as with any invoicing software — the office's CNPJ (or CPF) and municipal registration; the client's name, CPF or CNPJ, address, e-mail and phone; the service description, period, amounts, withholdings and tax classification. It is signed with the office's digital certificate.
  • When: only an invoice approved in Approvals, one by one. It starts in the government's restricted test environment (no tax value); production only after the office owner enables it.
  • Lookups: to know whether the municipality issues through the national standard (once a month), the status of an invoice and its events, PAULUS queries the National System with the office certificate. Once a month it also reads the public NFS-e documentation page on gov.br to learn about new schemas — read-only, nothing from the office is sent.
  • What stays on the computer: the XML of every declaration sent and every invoice received, event requests and events (cancellation, replacement), the PDF (DANFSe) and the tax breakdown, in PAULUS's data folder (included in the encrypted backup) with a copy in the document collection. An issued invoice is never deleted by PAULUS: tax law requires keeping it until the tax claims expire.
  • The certificate: a copy of the invoice .pfx stays in the data folder; the password is stored only if the office asks, encrypted by the Windows account (DPAPI). The private key is never written in clear, and the program's log file keeps from each request only the method, the address (without the invoice number) and the response code.
  • E-mail to the client and the accountant: PAULUS only proposes them, in Approvals, through the office's own e-mail account.
  • Tax advice: PAULUS does not give it. It applies the tax setup defined by the office (or its accountant); see the Terms of use.

Retention and deletion

  • The Google authorization is kept on the computer while the account is connected to PAULUS.
  • The log of cloud sends (with the text that left), the record of the yes and the encrypted keys stay on the computer, in the PAULUS data folder, until the office deletes them. What the website keeps about the cloud account is described in Cloud.
  • To disconnect: in PAULUS, open the Email module and remove the account. The stored authorization is deleted from the computer. To stop only Calendar, turn off sync in Settings › Connections.
  • Drive folder copies stay in the Archive, on your computer, while you keep following them. In Settings › Connections you can stop following a folder and, if you tick "Also delete the Archive copy", the copy is deleted from the computer.
  • Events PAULUS created in your Google Calendar and documents you uploaded to your Drive remain there after you disconnect, managed by Google; deleting them is your choice, in Google itself.
  • To revoke at Google: go to https://myaccount.google.com/permissions, choose PAVLVS and remove access. From then on the program can no longer read or send messages with that account.
  • Files you saved on the computer yourself (for example, downloaded attachments) remain in the folders where you saved them until you delete them.
  • Messages remain in your Gmail account, managed by Google.
  • From the former project support, which has been removed from PAULUS, the website may still hold, unpublished: each payment's status, amount, date and email fingerprint, which delete themselves within 400 days of the contribution; and each month's total and the names on the former supporters page, until they are deleted. To ask us to delete any data we hold, write to contato@paulus.ia.br.

Your rights (LGPD)

Under the LGPD (art. 18) you may request, among others: confirmation that your data is processed and access to it; correction; anonymization, blocking or deletion of unnecessary data; portability; information about who the data was shared with; information about the possibility of not consenting; and revocation of consent.

How to exercise them: write to contato@paulus.ia.br. Since the data processed by the program stays on your computer, you exercise most of these rights directly in it (removing the account, deleting files) and in your Google account. If your data is held by a law office that uses PAULUS, the request must be made to that office, which controls that data. You may also complain to Brazil's National Data Protection Authority (ANPD).
